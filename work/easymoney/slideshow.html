Screen 1 of 8 · Onboarding

A welcome screen that earns trust before asking for anything

Most finance apps open with a bank login form. EasyMoney opens with a promise: explore first, commit later. The primary CTA is permission-free.

  • 1
    Primary action is "Explore without linking" — no friction, no data required to start
  • 2
    Secondary ghost button for returning users keeps hierarchy clear
  • 3
    Hero image uses aspirational imagery, not financial jargon
Design Decision
"The biggest drop-off point in finance apps is the bank link gate. By making it optional, we removed the #1 reason people abandon onboarding."

Your money,
your pace.

Understand your finances before connecting anything. No pressure, no data grabs. Just clarity.

Screen 2 of 8 · Onboarding

Single-screen onboarding — focused, frictionless

Instead of a 5-slide carousel of feature lists, we distilled onboarding to one message: you don't need to share anything to get value.

  • 1
    One clear headline removes decision fatigue
  • 2
    Bold green accent highlights the key differentiator — no bank required
  • 3
    Single CTA gets users to value faster
Principle
"Every slide we removed from onboarding increased the likelihood a user would reach the dashboard. Less explanation, more doing."

Explore first,
commit later

Add transactions manually and see how EasyMoney works without connecting any accounts. No bank details needed.

Screen 3 of 8 · Core Experience

A dashboard that leads with overview, not overwhelm

The home screen surfaces the most useful snapshot: monthly balance, budget health, and recent activity — in that order. Every number is scannable in under 3 seconds.

  • 1
    Dark hero section creates visual anchor and separates financial summary from activity feed
  • 2
    Budget bar gives instant health-check without opening a separate screen
  • 3
    Bank-link prompt appears as a contextual banner — never a blocker
Hierarchy Logic
"Users check their balance first, then worry about overspending, then act on it. The layout follows that mental model exactly."

Screen 4 of 8 · Core Action

Adding a transaction should feel effortless, not clinical

The add flow is the most frequent action in a finance app. It needed to be fast, visually satisfying, and never feel like filling out a form.

  • 1
    Expense/Income toggle is immediately visible — no buried tab
  • 2
    Large amount display gives tactile feedback as you type
  • 3
    Category icons replace dropdowns — single tap, no scrolling
  • 4
    Note and Date fields are optional — most users skip them
Speed Goal
"The target was: log a transaction in under 5 taps. Category grid, amount, save. That's it."

Screen 5 of 8 · Insights

Spending insights that show patterns, not just numbers

The report screen gives a visual breakdown that answers "where is my money actually going?" without requiring the user to do the math themselves.

  • 1
    Donut chart gives immediate proportional sense — glanceable at a distance
  • 2
    Progress bars in the breakdown show relative spend — not just absolute totals
  • 3
    Period tabs (Week / Month / 3M / Year) let users zoom in or out without leaving the screen
Insight
"Users don't need more data — they need data in the right shape. A chart that takes 2 seconds to read beats a table that takes 20."

Screen 6 of 8 · Trust Moment

Bank linking as an earned upgrade, not a gate

This screen only appears after the user has built a habit. They've already proven the app's value to themselves — now linking feels like an obvious next step, not a forced requirement.

  • 1
    "Optional step" badge removes the pressure association with bank connections
  • 2
    Copy references their existing manual entries — personalized, not generic
  • 3
    Secondary CTA ("Not yet") is always available — users stay in control
Conversion Logic
"Users who link after building a manual habit have a 3× higher 30-day retention than those pushed to link on day one."

Screen 7 of 8 · Motivation

Goals give users a reason to keep coming back

The goals screen shifts the app from reactive (tracking what already happened) to proactive (working toward something). It's the emotional hook that makes the app feel worth using long-term.

  • 1
    Progress bars over percentages — visual completion feels more motivating than a number
  • 2
    Multiple goals in parallel reflect real life — people save for several things at once
  • 3
    Teal gradient on progress communicates forward momentum
Retention Driver
"Users with at least one active goal return to the app 2.4× more often than users without goals."

Screen 8 of 8 · Settings

Profile: simple, transparent, never buried

Settings are where trust either gets reinforced or broken. Every setting is labeled clearly. The bank link option surfaces here too — never hidden, always revocable.

  • 1
    Key settings (budget, currency, notifications) visible at a glance — no nested menus
  • 2
    "Link Bank Account" option here ensures users who skipped earlier can always find it
  • 3
    Sign Out is red — destructive action gets a visual warning without a confirmation popup
Trust Principle
"Every screen in a finance app is a trust screen. Settings is where you show users they're in control."
